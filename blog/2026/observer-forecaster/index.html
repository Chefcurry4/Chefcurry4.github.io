Part I

Novelty of the formalism

Whether the problem statement, the objective, or the architecture is new — judged against machine learning at large, not only against aerodynamics.
  • Q5

    Is the problem formulation new — observing one quantity and predicting a different one through a latent state?

    Observer theory, soft sensing, virtual sensing, and the closest instances outside fluids.
  • Q6

    Is the training objective new — forking a blind rollout from every filter state and scoring every lead?

    Overshooting, latent overshooting, forking sequences, value-equivalent models, and goal-oriented model reduction.
  • Q7

    Is the architecture new?

    Component by component. The answer is no, and the thesis is stronger for saying so first.
  • Q8

    Which papers are closest on formalism, and what exactly separates this model from each?

    With citations, links, and surviving differentiators.
  • Q9

    Composing one operator forty times, or training forty predictors — which is right?

    Direct versus recursive, and why the forecasting literature leans the other way.

Review

Extended literature review

A ten-lane adversarial sweep, every citation opened and quoted rather than remembered. The instruction to each lane was to find the paper that scoops this thesis.
  • L1

    How do you review a literature you are motivated to under-read?

    The method: parallel lanes, adversarial prompting, verbatim quotation, and a quarantine for anything that could not be opened.
  • L2

    Which claims died, and to which paper?

    The kill list, published openly, including the ones that were previously advertised as the contribution.
  • L3

    Which papers turned out to be gifts rather than threats?

    Prior work that names the objective and declines it, supplies a theorem, or publishes the negative result this thesis answers.
  • L4

    How many citations survive a check, and what does that say about citing from memory?

    Roughly forty-five live bibliographic traps: changed titles, one-digit DOIs, sentences deleted between preprint and journal, and one paper that does not exist.
  • L5

    Where is the real contribution, once the obvious candidates are gone?

    Forecasting, autoregressivity, and pressure-to-loads all need re-examining.

Part III

Why each design choice

Every component, interrogated for whether it earns its place — and what the evidence for it actually is.
  • Q14

    Why a recurrent gated cell, and not an LSTM, a state-space model, or a neural ODE?

    Including the uncomfortable answer about how many alternatives were actually trained.
  • Q15

    Why an encoder, a latent, and a decoder at all — could raw pressure be fed straight in?

    The measured collapse when the encoder is removed.
  • Q16

    Why is the encoder built from several parts, and is each one earning its place?

    Positional encoding, attention block, pooling — and which of them this recipe never exercises.
  • Q17

    Why a probabilistic decoder — what does uncertainty buy, and what does it not?

    What the band supports, and what it structurally cannot support.
  • Q18

    Why train on short bursts rather than whole trajectories?

    Coverage at equal compute, gust sampling bias, and which arguments are real versus reconstructed after the fact.
  • Q19

    Why is the latent state not initialised — and what does the burn-in cost at deployment?

    Washout, cold start, and an untested deployment mode.
  • Q20

    Why keep history in the state instead of feeding a window of past inputs?

    Delay embedding, and why the literature does not settle this in my favour.

Part IV

Model architecture

The full technical walkthrough: tensor path, objective, parameter budget, and the mechanics of the blind branch.
  • A1

    What happens to a single pressure reading on its way to a force?

    Tokenisation, set attention, pooling, recurrence, decoding — with shapes.
  • A2

    What exactly does the blind branch do, step by step?

    Deleting the input term, forking from every anchor, discarding the branch, keeping the gradient.
  • A3

    Where do the parameters actually live, and which of them do the forecasting?

    The share of the network that survives when the input pathway is switched off.
  • A4

    Which parts of the codebase are built, and which are dead in this configuration?

    An honest inventory, because several documented components are never constructed.

Part V

Limits and future work

Assumptions, measured failure modes, and the experiments that would settle what is still open.
  • Q21

    What does the application assume?

    Reynolds number, angle of attack, two dimensions, rigid and stationary body, one gust family, noise-free taps, fixed timestep.
  • Q22

    What does the architecture assume, and where do those assumptions bind?

    Markov latent, diagonal Gaussian, marginals rather than a joint, timestep baked in, exactly two components.
  • Q23

    How far does this generalise — other quantities, other observables, other systems?

    What transfers by construction, and the hard limits on extension.
  • Q24

    Does more data help? Does a bigger network?

    A measured scaling null, and where the capacity bottleneck actually sits.
  • Q25

    Where does the model fail, measured rather than assumed?

    Sensor brittleness, the reacquisition transient, phase-conditional calibration, and why drag is harder than lift.
  • Q26

    What does a sub-chord forecast actually buy, physically?

    Compared against real preview horizons from gust-load alleviation and wind energy.
  • Q27

    What happens with real, noisy, experimental pressure?

    The largest untested gap.
  • Q28

    What is the latent, physically — is it tracking the gust or the wake?

    Probes that are written but not yet run, and the drag paradox.
  • Q29

    Compared to what? What is the baseline, and has it been beaten fairly?

    Why the memoryless floor is a lead-zero baseline only.
  • Q30

    What would falsify this thesis, and which falsifiers are one run away?

    A pre-registered list, published before the runs that could settle it.
